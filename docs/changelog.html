1.2.0

2 August 2026 — dictation, finished properly

The record button becomes a button, and dictated words start behaving like words you typed.

Changed

  • The record button is a circle with a glyph in it — ● to record, ■ to stop — with the instruction beside it rather than inside it, so it stops changing width as you use it. Its colour follows your pointer as well as its state: hover it and it turns green; while it is recording, hovering it turns it red. Moving away puts it back without changing what it will do.
  • Dictated words go through the editor now. That means Ctrl+Z takes back one thing you said rather than the whole session, text you type while recording is kept instead of being overwritten by the next thing you say, and words land where your cursor is rather than always at the end.

Fixed

  • Dictation said it was ready whenever the speech-model folder existed, even if the model inside was missing or half-copied — a record button that could only fail when pressed. It now checks the one file the recogniser cannot start without.
  • On an install under a drive-letterless volume (a folder-mounted drive), the model path handed to the recogniser was turned into one that named nothing at all. It now names the model — and if loading it still fails there, the error says which model it could not open rather than quietly looking somewhere else.
  • Dictation could report itself unavailable while a perfectly good model was installed: a stray or half-copied vosk-model-en in your data folder was preferred over the one inside the app, and then rejected for being incomplete. An unusable folder is now passed over.

Documentation

  • Search on this site finds dictation — it shipped in 1.1.0 and was missing from the index. Results that could only ever 404 are gone at the same time: the shared index listed five Freally apps that have no site yet. The third-party notices that ship in the installer now record Vosk and its Windows runtime alongside the fonts.
  • The dictation note in Settings says what happens with the LAN mirror on: dictated text reaches its viewers as it is written, the same as anything you type.

1.1.0

2 August 2026 — dictation

One way to use your voice, and it works the moment you switch it on.

Added

  • Write your script by speaking. Turn on Dictation in Settings → Voice and a record button appears above the script. Press it and what you say is written in; press it again to stop. There is nothing to train and nothing to set up — the speech model ships inside the installer, so it works the first time you switch it on. Nothing is downloaded at runtime and nothing is sent anywhere; it works with the network cable pulled out. The microphone is open only while recording, and the only thing ever written down is the text you see appear.

Removed

  • Voice commands. They required you to record every command — play, pause, faster, slower, next pause, back to top — in your own voice before any of them did anything, and then to hold a button down while speaking. Until you had done that recording, holding the button opened the microphone and achieved nothing at all, with no way to tell that apart from a fault. Dictation covers the same ground with nothing to train.
  • Follow my reading. Gone for the same reason: one clear way to use your voice is better than three that overlap.

The installers are larger than 1.0.0's — the speech model is about 40 MB, and it now ships whether or not you use it.

1.0.0

2 August 2026 — the first published release

Run the prompter by voice, with commands you train in your own voice — plus a welcome tour, a theme that follows your system, and a keyboard accessibility pass. This is the first build you can download and install, signed, for Windows, macOS and Linux.

Added

  • A one-minute welcome tour. The first time you open the app, four short steps introduce the editor and its pause marks, the pace controls, and the projector. Skip it at any point, and run it again whenever you like from Settings → General → Show the tour again.
  • A theme that follows your system. Alongside Dark and Light there is now Same as my system, which switches with your operating system and repaints straight away when your machine changes at sunset. Dark is still the default.
  • Voice commands. Train play, pause, faster, slower, next pause and back to top in your own voice — a few takes each — and drive the prompter hands-free. A red ● Listening indicator shows whenever the microphone is open, and you choose push-to-talk or always-listening. Off by default. Model-free — matched against your own recordings on your machine.
  • Follow my reading. An opt-in mode that scrolls the script to keep up as you read it aloud, recognising the script's own words on your device with a bundled Apache-2.0 Vosk model. It steps aside to manual the moment it loses your place — so it never scrolls somewhere wrong — and drives only the operator preview, never the projector. Off by default, and greyed out where the model isn't installed.
  • Installers, signed and self-updating. Windows (.exe and .msi), a macOS universal .dmg, and Linux AppImage and .deb, all built from one tagged commit. The in-app updater checks this site's releases, and every update is verified against a signing key the app carries — an update that cannot be verified is refused rather than installed.

Changed

  • Keyboard and screen-reader accessibility pass. Every focusable control now shows a clear focus ring; Tab stays inside an open dialog instead of wandering onto the window behind it; closing a dialog hands focus back to the button that opened it; and the Settings category list reports the pane you are actually looking at, even while a search is filtering it. The app honours reduce motion throughout — the script's own scroll is deliberately left alone, because that is the thing the app is for and you already set its speed directly.

Privacy

  • Voice commands never touch the network or disk. They are matched by classic signal processing against your own recordings, on your machine — no model, no training data, no service. Audio is processed in memory only; nothing you say is written to a file.

Follow my reading is not usable in this build. The recogniser needs a speech model and this release does not yet bundle one, so the feature detects that and reports itself unavailable with a reason rather than failing when you switch it on. Voice commands are unaffected — they need no model and work here.

0.300.0

22 July 2026 — offline autocomplete

The editor finishes your words as you type, in any of the 18 languages — from word lists that live inside the app.

Added

  • Suggestions as you type. Start a word and the rest of it appears dimmed ahead of the cursor. Press Tab to take it, Esc to dismiss it, or just keep typing to ignore it. Finish a word and press space and it will offer to complete a whole common phrase.
  • Its own language setting. A new Editor pane in Settings turns suggestions on or off and picks which language they come from — separate from the app's own language, because writing a script in one language while running the app in another is completely normal.
  • All 18 languages, offline. About 464,000 words and 345,000 phrases ship inside the app. Only the language you are using is loaded.

Changed

  • The language list starts with English, then runs alphabetically by each language's own name — Deutsch, Español, Français, and so on — and stays in that order whichever language the app is in.

Privacy

  • Autocomplete never touches the network. It is a straight lookup against word lists inside the installer — no model, no service, no telemetry. Nothing you type leaves your machine, and it works with the computer offline.

0.200.0

21 July 2026 — the standalone teleprompter

Scripts you can keep, a projector for the talent, a real transport, and a prompter that reads itself aloud.

Added

  • A script library. Create, open, rename and delete scripts, with a recent list and autosave while you type. They are saved as plain text files, so a script of yours stays readable in any editor — even if this app disappears tomorrow. The app reopens whatever you had open last.
  • Pause chips in the editor. Every inline -- pause shows as a small pill with its length. It behaves as one thing: the cursor steps over it, Backspace removes the whole pause in one press, and copying it gives you back real -- text. Type two dashes and one appears.
  • A projector window for the talent. Put the reading surface on any connected display, filling it edge to edge, with a mirror flip for beam-splitter prompter glass. It scrolls off the same state as your preview, so the two can never disagree, and it has its own transport and keyboard shortcuts for whoever is reading.
  • Mirror to a phone or tablet. Show the scrolling script on any device on your own network, from a link and QR code the app gives you — each device picks its own font size without changing what anyone else sees. It is off by default, stays on this computer until you explicitly open it to the network, and is read-only: nothing on a phone can start, stop, or edit anything.
  • A proper transport. Play, pause, stop, rewind and fast-forward as real buttons that repeat when held; a seek bar showing elapsed and total reading time, with a preview of the words at any point; click a word to start there; and mousewheel scrubbing.
  • Speed in BPM. Read the pace as characters per second, or as BPM (20–250) if you are pacing to music.
  • Appearance controls. Typeface, weight, text colour, side margins, line spacing, and where the reading guide sits on screen — applied to every surface at once.
  • Read aloud. Have the prompter read your script at the pace you have set, to check timing before a take. Pauses are real silences, and the highlight follows the spoken word. It uses the voices your computer already has — nothing is downloaded — and it never moves the talent's projector.
  • Every language renders, on every machine. The app now carries the fonts for all 18 languages it speaks, including Arabic, Hindi, Japanese, Korean and Chinese. Switching language on a computer that has no font for it used to show rows of empty boxes; now it just reads.

Changed

  • The app draws its own window. A centred title, with Settings, About and the minimise / maximise / close buttons on the right — matching the other Freally apps. It still drags, double-clicks to maximise, and resizes from any edge or corner.
  • A proper Settings dialog, with a category sidebar (General, Reading, Appearance, Projector, Network), a search box, and OK / Cancel / Apply.
  • Minimize to the system tray, off by default. With it on, minimising tucks the window into the tray; restoring brings it back and removes the tray icon, so it is never in both places at once.
  • The app icon is now the same artwork at every size. Small icons used to show a cropped "F" instead; one consistent mark reads better in the taskbar.

Still no AI, no accounts, no telemetry. The network mirror serves your script to devices you point at it, on your own network. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is relayed through anyone's server.

0.100.0

21 July 2026 — the scaffold phase

The app now builds, runs, and gates on the agreement, with the character-based scroll engine ported over from Freally Capture.

Added

  • The character-based teleprompter engine. Scroll position is a real reading pace measured in characters per second, with inline -- pauses (--2 holds for two seconds), seek and step controls, and an optional countdown before the scroll begins. The preview and the projector lay text on one fixed-width stage, so they wrap at the identical column whatever the window size.
  • Single-window app shell with a script box and the live prompter surface, plus Settings that only take effect when you press Apply — cancelling restores exactly what you had.
  • 18 languages, following your system language by default.
  • First-run agreement. The app asks once, remembers your acceptance, and never loses it when you save settings.
  • Problem reporter and update check. If the app ever stops unexpectedly it writes a report locally and offers to send it — by GitHub issue, Gmail, or your own mail app. Nothing is ever sent without your click, and you read the exact text first.
  • App icons for Windows, macOS and Linux. Small sizes use a glyph-only "F", because the full wordmark is unreadable below 64px.

No AI, no accounts, no telemetry. The only request Freally Teleprompt makes is the update check, and it sends nothing about you, your machine, or your scripts.

Installers are published from 1.0.0 onward

0.100.0 through 0.300.0 were development milestones with no downloadable build. 1.0.0 is the first release you can install — Windows, macOS, and Linux, free, local-first, no account. Get it from the Releases page, and see the documentation for what the app does.
